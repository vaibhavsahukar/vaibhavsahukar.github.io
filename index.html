Options Bot

An unattended Python bot that trades 0DTE QQQ iron condors on Alpaca, across two paper accounts and one small live account.

  • –Chosen after testing 13 other strategy ideas on real option prices, under rules fixed in advance: a locked holdout year and about 20 variants per idea at most
  • –Sells a same-day-expiring QQQ iron condor twice a day, skipping any day where the credit is under 15¢
  • –Stops out at 2.5× the credit and force-closes any side near the price before the close; far sides expire worthless
  • –Live account needs an explicit opt-in, a 1-contract cap and a kill-switch file, with credentials kept apart from the paper accounts
  • –A reconciliation job replaces the bot’s own P&L estimates with real broker fills after the logs overstated results
  • –Backtested on two years of real 5-minute option trade prices with slippage scenarios; the sample of live days is still small

Reckless Driving: The Game

An endless arcade traffic game. Weave through dense highway traffic at triple-digit speeds, chain near-misses and cut-ups into combo multipliers — while the cops try to catch you.

A game with a cause: I built RDTG after witnessing a serious crash that totaled my family’s car and almost hurt my baby brother. Please consider donating to Families for Safe Streets or MADD, the causes this game was made to support.
  • –Three maps — City Highway, Countryside Highway, Forest Backroad — each with adjustable time of day and weather
  • –Six vehicles, cars and motorcycles, each with its own handling, wheelies and burnouts
  • –A wanted-star police system — patrol cars, bike cops and interceptors scale up the chase
  • –Physics-based crashes, fuel management, and four difficulty levels from Easy to Insane

In game, when you crash, the game exemplifies you by highlighting the potential damage you could have done if that crash was in real life, Nice one, that guy was going to propose tonight. — An example of the sarcastic, dark jokes included in game to highlight the real dangers of reckless driving.

manu ai

A garment is really a sourcing decision under constraints — budget, order size, skill required. manu ai underwrites that decision the way a screener prices a position: one design, ~40 candidate manufacturers, one weighted score per candidate. Built out of the exact problem Vain Apparel ran into — finding a factory for a design.

  • –Describe the garment or drop in a sketch — an LLM extracts the structured attributes once: fabric, construction complexity, special features
  • –Every manufacturer is scored on the same five weighted factors — specialty fit, capability match, MOQ fit, skill-vs-complexity, and budget tier — then ranked, not just filtered
  • –Each ranked pick carries its reasoning, so the shortlist reads like an allocation memo, not a black-box score
  • –Runs free by default (Groq); upgrades to Claude for vision + structured output only if you choose to spend on it

wtffdffmb

Who the fucking fuck doesn’t fucking follow me back: a Chrome extension that shows who you follow on Instagram that doesn’t follow you back, and can unfollow them for you.

  • –Chrome extension (Manifest V3, about 600 lines, no libraries) that lists who you follow that doesn’t follow you back, and can unfollow them
  • –A draggable bubble opens a step-by-step panel: go to your profile, scan Followers, scan Following, then review the list
  • –Scans by scrolling Instagram’s lists and recording every profile as it passes, since rows are removed once off-screen; skips “Suggested for you”
  • –Compares what it scanned with the count on your profile, and offers to scan again if it came up under 97%
  • –Unfollow all runs one account at a time with a pause you set (3 to 30 seconds), a warning that faster risks a ban, and a Stop button
  • –Everything is stored in your own browser and nothing is sent anywhere; a Vercel site hosts the guide and the download

PinPlan

It’s hard to find real information on steroids and peptides. PinPlan puts the actual data — compounds, dosing, half-lives, cited studies — in one place, instead of scattered forums and secondhand advice.

  • –A growing catalog of steroids, peptides and ancillaries, each with dosing and half-life data pulled from cited studies
  • –PinPlanner: drag compounds onto a canvas to build a stack, scored for synergy and safety against your own age, height and weight
  • –PinPal, an AI chat scoped to the catalog, for questions a static page can’t answer
  • –Every compound page cites its sources — no half-remembered forum advice
